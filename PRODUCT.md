# KnicksIQ

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Knicks fans who want to talk about the 2025-26 season. The conversation can use relevant NBA history and player context. It does not claim to know live news.

## Product purpose

Have a fun conversation with a knowledgeable, opinionated Knicks fan. Discuss the season, debate a take, compare players, explore a hypothetical, or play a requested quiz inside the same chat.

The public product is the conversation. It is not an archive browser, statistical dashboard, report viewer, or research interface.

## Conversation

Use a conversational voice. Be funny when appropriate and willing to disagree. Keep replies concise unless the person asks for depth. Ask a follow-up when it helps, not at the end of every answer.

Opinions and interpretations are welcome. Do not invent numbers, plays, quotes, or live information. Check concrete facts with internal read-only data when needed. If a detail is uncertain, say so naturally without refusing the whole topic or reciting archive limitations.

Welcome greetings and incidental small talk. Redirect unrelated work toward basketball. Decline unsafe requests. Do not become a general-purpose coding or research assistant.

## Capabilities and constraints

Open directly into chat with a visible composer and a few inviting starters. Show genuine incremental replies where appropriate. Do not simulate token streaming from a buffered answer. Do not require a separate model to approve every joke or opinion.

Refresh resumes the current browser tab's conversation. New chat starts fresh. Do not add accounts, a history sidebar, or a long-term personal profile.

Quizzes, rankings, and other playful activities happen inside chat when requested. Do not add separate modes or screens.

Remove public archive, game, report, and source views and public game/report data exports. Old application page URLs return to chat. Keep the information available to internal code. Privacy, terms, and feedback remain accessible through quiet utilities.

Do not show report panels, evidence cards, internal receipts, tool traces, database terminology, or infrastructure status in the conversation interface. Do not fabricate data coverage or deployment claims.

## Brand commitments

Keep the KnicksIQ name, existing mark, Knicks blue, and Knicks orange. Use a fixed light neutral presentation. Do not retain the cream editorial design or follow the device theme. Keep the conversation more prominent than branding or controls.

KnicksIQ is an unofficial fan project with no NBA or Knicks affiliation.

Use the React adapter and public behaviors from https://github.com/vercel-labs/ui for the interface. The existing application uses React, TypeScript, Vite, and a Python API.

## Evidence on hand

The backend has release-scoped game and player statistics, play-by-play, reviewed reports, and read-only retrieval and calculation tools. Available data coverage remains a data fact, not a product promise.

Existing session, replay, request-limit, and spending safeguards protect the chat. Their internal implementation must not dictate an archive-style public experience.

The approved product interview and implementation request replace the earlier archive brief. The prior pushed chat branch remains intact as a record of the rejected direction.

## Product principles

- Let the person talk naturally about basketball.
- Use facts quietly to support the conversation.
- Prefer a useful honest answer over a formal limitation message.
- Keep the public interface focused on the current conversation.
